## Scenarios

A scenario is a named selection of a dataflow's nodes, in "dataflow"."scenarios", used to compare alternatives: the same analysis with buildings twice as tall, say. Its boundary splits the dataflow into three parts. Its fixed context is the nodes outside it that it reads, through an edge into it or through a {{template.label:curio.builtin/parameter}} node's reference in its code. Its levers are its own nodes: what an alternative changes, such as a widget value, a line of code or a spec. Its outcomes are the outputs of its last nodes: what gets compared. A node belongs to one scenario at most, so a node two alternatives share sits outside both, as their common context. An alternative is usually a copy of another scenario's nodes that reads the same context, with a lever changed; each copy names the nodes it descends from in "metadata"."copiedFrom", which is how the levers of two scenarios pair.

To compare scenarios, connect each scenario's outcome to an input circle of its own, on a node outside the boxes. A {{template.label:curio.builtin/vis-vega}} node whose spec reads its inputs stacked charts them: "data": {"name": "{{inputs.stacked}}"} is every input's rows stacked, each row with "scenario" and "scenario_name" from the scenario box its input comes from. A chart or a map draws exactly its spec, and its scenario chips write the boxes' colors and order, from the boxes its inputs come from: "scale": {{scenarios.chip:colors}} on a color, fill or stroke on "scenario_name" (on a text mark, "scale": {{scenarios.chip:text_colors}}, the same colors in shades that read on white), and "sort": {{scenarios.chip:order}} on an axis, an offset or a facet on it, for example "color": {"field": "scenario_name", "type": "nominal", "scale": {{scenarios.chip:colors}}} and "x": {"field": "scenario_name", "type": "nominal", "sort": {{scenarios.chip:order}}}. Each input whose node is in no box comes last, in a grey of its own, and when no input comes from a box the colors chips write the names alone, so the grammar's own palette colors them. In a map's layer, or a chart's mark, "{{scenarios.chip:input.1}}" inside quotes is the color of the box input_1 comes from: "color": "{{scenarios.chip:input.1}}". A spec without the chips draws in Vega-Lite's or Autark's own colors, and the chips are for a spec, not for Python or JavaScript code. A {{template.label:curio.scout/raster-calculator}} node takes the difference of two rasters cell by cell, raster_calculate("subtract", [input_1, input_0], output_file=curio_output_file), with absolute=True for its size. A {{template.label:curio.builtin/computation-analysis}} node takes the difference of two tables or layers, joining their rows on a column both have, such as osm_id or building_id.

The Scout package (curio.scout@1) has fifteen charts that compare scenarios: {{template.label:curio.scout/lines-with-band}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/curves-by-group}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/bars-with-error-bars}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/ensemble-strips}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/stacked-bars-with-totals}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/donuts}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/weighted-matrix}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/dots-with-a-range}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/bars}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/grouped-bars}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/lines}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/points}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/pie}}, {{template.label:curio.scout/lollipops}} and {{template.label:curio.scout/table}}. Each takes one input per scenario: connect each scenario's outcome to a circle of its own. Its spec reads them all stacked, as "data": {"name": "{{inputs.stacked}}"}, every input's rows without their geometry, each row with "scenario" and "scenario_name" from the scenario box its input comes from, and its scenario chips draw each scenario in its box's color, in the order the dataflow lists the boxes. Each is a Vega-Lite node whose spec is its template's: set the columns it plots in its widgets, and its Combine widget, where it has one, to the Vega-Lite aggregate it combines a scenario's rows with (mean, sum, median, min, max or count), and leave its spec as it is.
