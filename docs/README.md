## Examples

Each example below has a JSON dataflow you can import into Curio plus a step-by-step markdown walkthrough. Pipeline overviews in the walkthroughs are drawn with [Mermaid](https://mermaid.js.org/) `flowchart` blocks, which GitHub renders inline.

The same examples are also seeded into the public deployments at [**flow.urbantk.org**](https://flow.urbantk.org) (stable) and [**dev.urbantk.org**](https://dev.urbantk.org) (latest `main`). Sign in to fork them into your own projects, or browse them read-only as a guest.

Every example reads its inputs from the [Data Catalog](DATA-CATALOG.md): the datasets ship in `<repo_root>/datasets/` and each dataflow declares the ones it needs, so the loader nodes address them by id with `curio_data_path("<id>")`, or `curio_load_collection("<id>")` for a collection. The storage examples (18 to 23) read datasets added from the **Example storage** source of the [Discovery Catalog](DISCOVERY-CATALOG.md). The four Autark examples (06, 07, 08, 11) read a committed `.osm.pbf` by relative path instead, because the browser fetches those bytes directly and `.pbf` is not a catalog format.

Icons indicate the complexity level of each example: 🟢 Easy, 🟡 Intermediate, 🔴 Advanced.

### SCOUT scenario studies

Four test dataflows run SCOUT's studies as scenarios and compare them. They have no walkthrough: open them from **Tests**, under **By source**, on the projects page, and read their nodes in the page linked in their row.

| Dataflow | Functionality | Use case | Described in |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Quad Cities flood depth, nature-based solutions and vulnerability](examples/dataflows/quad-cities-flood-vulnerability.json) | Curio's own nodes and Scout's `Raster Calculator` (`curio.scout@1`): three `Parameter` nodes (the period and the region's two corners, SCOUT's flood example's), `Data Loading` nodes that read the region's window of SCOUT's flood rasters, and in each scenario, No NbS and NbS, a `Raster Calculator` that takes the depth with nature-based solutions where the class is a chosen one, a second that takes the depth's statistics and an Autark map of the depth; another `Raster Calculator` takes the size of the change in depth, which an Autark map draws, and Scout's `Bars` charts the median depth. Two more `Data Loading` nodes read CDC/ATSDR's Social Vulnerability Index for the census tracts and USGS's rivers, lakes and reservoirs, a `Data Transformation` keeps the tracts the rivers touch, which an Autark map draws by their SVI percentile, and in each scenario a `Data Transformation` averages the depth over each tract's land, the water left out, which an Autark map draws, and a third `Raster Calculator` averages it over the riverside land in each period; a `Python Computation` node takes the size of the change in each tract's mean depth, matched by the tract's FIPS code, which an Autark map draws, and Scout's `Points` and `Grouped bars` chart vulnerability against depth and the mean depth by period | Nature-based flood mitigation in SCOUT's Quad Cities flood projections, weighed against social vulnerability on the riverside tracts | [Rasters](USAGE.md#rasters) |
| [Lower Manhattan park shade with towers removed or capped](examples/dataflows/lower-manhattan-park-shade.json) | An `autk-grammar` node loads OpenStreetMap's parks of Lower Manhattan and the buildings of a box about 300 m larger from `lower_mnt.osm.pbf`; `Python Computation` nodes give every building part a height, keep the open-air parks and mark the parts at least the Tall building height tall within the Distance from a park of any park (two `Parameter` nodes, 60 m and 75 m). In three scenarios, Existing, Towers removed and Height cap, whose `Interaction` nodes remove the marked parts or cut them to the Tall building height, `Rasterize Buildings` and `Accumulated Shadow` give the shadow over the season of a third `Parameter` node, a `Python Computation` node measures it in each park, and Autark maps draw the buildings in 3D and the shadow. Scout's `Dots with a range` charts each park's mean minutes of shadow, each scenario on an input of its own, and for each what-if a `Raster Calculator` takes the change in shadow, cell by cell, and a `Python Computation` node the change per park, matched by the park's name, which Autark maps draw | Shade in the parks of the Financial District and City Hall with the towers around them removed or capped | [`model.scout.deep-umbra@1`](../packages/model.scout.deep-umbra@1/README.md) |
| [Chicago Loop shadow with towers removed or edited](examples/dataflows/chicago-loop-shadow-towers.json) | Example 24's `Rasterize Buildings`, `Accumulated Shadow`, Autark map of the shadow and `Raster Calculator` of its statistics in two scenarios over a shared season, Existing and Towers removed, whose `Interaction` node takes 15 buildings out by their `building_id` and can remove, raise or lower more; Scout's `Bars` charts both scenarios' mean accumulated shadow, each on an input of its own, and a third `Raster Calculator` takes Towers removed's shadow minus Existing's, cell by cell, which an Autark map draws | High-rise shadows in SCOUT's Chicago Loop example | [`model.scout.deep-umbra@1`](../packages/model.scout.deep-umbra@1/README.md) |
| [Routing around rain and wind in downtown Chicago](examples/dataflows/chicago-rain-wind-routing.json) | SCOUT's weather routing example: `Weather Routing` (`model.scout.weather-gnn@1`) over the roads of SCOUT's box in downtown Chicago from an Autark node, and two scenarios, Fastest route and Weather-aware route, that each keep one of its routes; an Autark map draws both routes in their scenarios' colors, and four of Scout's `Bars` charts (`curio.scout@1`), each taking both scenarios' metrics on inputs of their own, compare their duration, distance and rain and wind exposure | Weather-aware routing with SCOUT's WRF forecast and weather graph network | [`model.scout.weather-gnn@1`](../packages/model.scout.weather-gnn@1/README.md) |
